# Vitamin C-Rich Fruits: 8 Options and How Much They Provide

When people think of **vitamin C-rich fruits**, oranges are often the first food that comes to mind. Oranges certainly provide vitamin C, but they are not the only option — and they are not necessarily the richest source.

Acerola, cashew apple, guava, papaya, kiwi and strawberries can all provide substantial amounts. Some contain considerably more vitamin C per 100 grams than oranges. ([TBCA](https://www.tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=MHB%2FMSN8lxZs9gJPLzL%2FMg%3D%3D&utm_source=chatgpt.com 'Malpighia spp.'))

Rather than focusing on a single "best" fruit, dietary variety is usually the more practical approach.

## What does vitamin C do?

Vitamin C, also known as ascorbic acid, is a water-soluble nutrient involved in several important physiological processes.

According to the NIH, it:

- is required for collagen production;
- acts as an antioxidant;
- supports normal immune function;
- improves absorption of iron from plant foods;
- contributes to processes involved in wound healing. ([ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-Consumer/ 'Vitamin C - Consumer'))

These roles make adequate intake important, but more vitamin C does not automatically mean proportionally greater health benefits.

## How much vitamin C do adults need?

The reference values presented by the NIH include:

| Group                | Vitamin C per day |
| -------------------- | ----------------- |
| Adult men            | 90 mg             |
| Adult women          | 75 mg             |
| Pregnant adults      | 85 mg             |
| Breastfeeding adults | 120 mg            |

People who smoke require an additional **35 mg per day** under these reference values. ([ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-Consumer/ 'Vitamin C - Consumer'))

Individual needs can differ, so these figures should be viewed as population-level reference intakes rather than personalized prescriptions.

## Which fruits are high in vitamin C?

![A selection of vitamin C-rich fruits, including acerola, cashew apple, guava, papaya, kiwi, strawberries, oranges and pineapple.](/src/assets/uploads/frutas-com-vitamina-c-01.png 'A selection of vitamin C-rich fruits, including acerola, cashew apple, guava, papaya, kiwi, strawberries, oranges and pineapple.')

The following values come from Brazil's **TBCA food composition database** and refer to approximately 100 g of the edible portion of specific fruit entries.

| Fruit             | Approximate vitamin C per 100 g |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Ripe acerola pulp | 1,505 mg                        |
| Cashew apple      | 257 mg                          |
| Guava             | 89.9 mg                         |
| Formosa papaya    | 78.5 mg                         |
| Kiwi              | 70.8 mg                         |
| Strawberries      | 69.8 mg                         |
| Pera orange       | 52.2 mg                         |
| Pineapple         | 33.1 mg                         |

Food composition is naturally variable, so these figures should not be interpreted as fixed values for every cultivar or individual fruit. ([TBCA](https://www.tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=MHB%2FMSN8lxZs9gJPLzL%2FMg%3D%3D&utm_source=chatgpt.com 'Malpighia spp.'))

## 1. Acerola

Acerola, sometimes called Barbados cherry, is one of the standout fruits for vitamin C.

One TBCA entry for ripe acerola pulp contains approximately **1,505 mg per 100 g**. Other samples show different concentrations, illustrating why food composition figures should always be interpreted as estimates rather than universal constants. ([TBCA](https://www.tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=MHB%2FMSN8lxZs9gJPLzL%2FMg%3D%3D&utm_source=chatgpt.com 'Malpighia spp.'))

Even a relatively small amount can therefore provide significant vitamin C.

## 2. Cashew apple

The **cashew apple** is the fleshy fruit attached to the cashew nut and is especially common in Brazil and other tropical regions.

TBCA data list approximately **257 mg of vitamin C per 100 g** of raw cashew apple pulp. ([TBCA](https://www.tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=NgpouW7R2wuRq%2FCG8yt4%2FQ%3D%3D&utm_source=chatgpt.com '..:: TBCA - Composição de Alimentos (Em Medidas ...'))

This makes it one of the most concentrated options included in this comparison.

## 3. Guava

Guava is another useful source.

An average of different Brazilian samples in the TBCA provides approximately **89.9 mg per 100 g** — almost exactly the NIH reference intake of 90 mg for an adult man and more than the 75 mg reference for an adult woman. ([TBCA](https://tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=%2B3p%2FYz%2FKFQJlzFNntQLS1A%3D%3D '..:: TBCA - Composição de Alimentos (Em Medidas Caseiras) ::..'))

The same TBCA entry also contains approximately 5.78 g of dietary fiber per 100 g. ([TBCA](https://tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=%2B3p%2FYz%2FKFQJlzFNntQLS1A%3D%3D '..:: TBCA - Composição de Alimentos (Em Medidas Caseiras) ::..'))

## 4. Papaya

Papaya demonstrates that a fruit does not need to taste particularly acidic to contain meaningful amounts of vitamin C.

Raw Formosa papaya in the TBCA provides approximately **78.5 mg per 100 g**. A 170 g portion in the same entry provides about 133 mg. ([TBCA](https://tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=N%2BgREPajSjWOCcTNi5hX7g%3D%3D '..:: TBCA - Composição de Alimentos (Em Medidas Caseiras) ::..'))

## 5. Kiwi

Raw kiwi provides approximately **70.8 mg of vitamin C per 100 g** in the Brazilian database. ([TBCA](https://www.tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=K5WD7sv2VBQFAmPbA1F7Rg%3D%3D '..:: TBCA - Composição de Alimentos (Em Medidas Caseiras) ::..'))

It can be eaten on its own or combined with yogurt, oats and other fruits.

## 6. Strawberries

Strawberries are another non-citrus source of vitamin C.

An average of different Brazilian samples in the TBCA contains approximately **69.8 mg per 100 g**. A 120 g serving provides roughly 83.8 mg according to the same entry. ([TBCA](https://www.tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=b7WfIT%2FR72TNSHwxAYkp5A%3D%3D '..:: TBCA - Composição de Alimentos (Em Medidas Caseiras) ::..'))

## 7. Oranges

Oranges deserve their reputation as a source of vitamin C, even though they do not top this list.

Brazilian Pera orange contains approximately **52.2 mg per 100 g**, while the 140 g medium portion listed by the TBCA provides around 73.1 mg. ([TBCA](https://www.tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=XYjI%2FfkZDOQ%2FtRn1x1N8tQ%3D%3D '..:: TBCA - Composição de Alimentos (Em Medidas Caseiras) ::..'))

That still makes oranges a convenient way to add vitamin C to the diet.

## 8. Pineapple

Pineapple provides a more moderate concentration but can still contribute meaningfully.

Raw pineapple contains approximately **33.1 mg per 100 g** in the TBCA entry used here. ([TBCA](https://www.tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=lK2EzSMttYkJXEBRRJ2pGA%3D%3D '..:: TBCA - Composição de Alimentos (Em Medidas Caseiras) ::..'))

Combined with other fruits and vegetables eaten throughout the day, it can help support adequate intake.

## Are oranges the best source of vitamin C?

Not if vitamin C concentration per 100 g is the only criterion.

In the Brazilian entries reviewed, acerola and cashew apple contain far more vitamin C than Pera orange. Guava, papaya, kiwi and strawberries also rank above it in this particular comparison. ([TBCA](https://www.tbca.net.br/base-dados/int_composicao_alimentos.php?n0REd3kv7e86D%2BViXWYUnQ%3D%3D=MHB%2FMSN8lxZs9gJPLzL%2FMg%3D%3D&utm_source=chatgpt.com 'Malpighia spp.'))

In everyday life, however, availability, affordability, seasonality, enjoyment and overall dietary variety also matter.

## Whole fruit or fruit juice?

![Whole fruits beside a glass of freshly squeezed juice in a bright kitchen.](/src/assets/uploads/frutas-com-vitamina-c-02.png 'Whole fruits beside a glass of freshly squeezed juice in a bright kitchen.')

Whenever practical, **whole fruit is a strong default choice**.

Whole fruit provides fiber along with vitamins, minerals, water and other food components. Brazil's Ministry of Health encourages the regular consumption of varied fresh fruits. ([Serviços e Informações do Brasil](https://www.gov.br/saude/pt-br/assuntos/saude-brasil/eu-quero-me-alimentar-melhor/noticias/2022/frutas-in-natura-sao-sempre-a-melhor-opcao?utm_source=chatgpt.com 'Frutas in natura são sempre a melhor opção - Portal Gov.br'))

The WHO also notes that most fruit juices — including those with no added sugar — contain significant amounts of free sugars and recommends limiting their consumption. ([Organização Mundial da Saúde](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet?utm_source=chatgpt.com 'Healthy diet'))

This does not mean fruit juice has to be completely avoided, but it does not need to routinely replace whole fruit.

## Does storage affect vitamin C?

Yes.

The NIH notes that prolonged storage and cooking can reduce the amount of vitamin C in food. Many vitamin C-rich fruits have the advantage of being commonly eaten raw. ([ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-Consumer/ 'Vitamin C - Consumer'))

Freshness and preparation methods can therefore influence actual intake.

## Vitamin C and iron absorption

![A meal of beans and vegetables served with fresh guava and orange.](/src/assets/uploads/frutas-com-vitamina-c-03.webp 'A meal of beans and vegetables served with fresh guava and orange.')

Vitamin C improves the absorption of **non-heme iron**, the form found in plant foods. ([ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-Consumer/ 'Vitamin C - Consumer'))

Pairing beans, lentils or other plant-based sources of iron with guava, orange, acerola, cashew apple, papaya or another vitamin C-rich food can therefore be a useful meal-planning strategy.

## Do you need a vitamin C supplement?

Not automatically.

A varied diet containing fruits and vegetables can provide meaningful amounts of vitamin C, and these foods are among the nutrient's main dietary sources. ([ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-Consumer/ 'Vitamin C - Consumer'))

High supplemental doses should not be taken simply under the assumption that more is always better. The NIH lists a tolerable upper intake level of **2,000 mg per day for adults** from all sources. Excessive intakes can cause diarrhea, nausea and abdominal cramps. ([ODS](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-Consumer/ 'Vitamin C - Consumer'))

People with medical conditions, medication use or suspected deficiency should seek individualized professional guidance before using high-dose supplements.

## Conclusion

There are plenty of **vitamin C-rich fruits** beyond oranges.

Acerola and cashew apple are exceptionally concentrated examples, while guava, papaya, kiwi, strawberries, oranges and pineapple can all contribute to daily intake.

Rather than relying on a single food, choose a variety of whole fruits, make use of seasonal produce and build vitamin C intake into an overall balanced eating pattern.
