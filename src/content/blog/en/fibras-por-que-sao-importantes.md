Fiber is the part of plant food that the human intestine does not digest. Precisely because it is not digested, it influences intestinal transit, satiety, glucose absorption, and the microbiota — four effects that accumulate over the years.

## The two types

**Soluble fiber** forms a gel when it comes into contact with water. This gel slows gastric emptying, smooths the glucose curve after a meal, and helps reduce LDL cholesterol. It is found in oats, apples, carrots, legumes, and chia and flax seeds.

**Insoluble fiber** does not form a gel: it increases stool bulk and speeds up intestinal transit. It is found in wheat bran, fruit and vegetable peels, leafy vegetables, and whole grains.

:::nota[There is no need to choose]
Real foods almost always contain both types in different proportions. Eating a varied diet balances them without the need for calculations.
:::

## What fiber does

- **Satiety:** increases the volume of the meal and prolongs the feeling of fullness, which helps with weight control
- **Blood glucose:** reduces the speed of glucose absorption and smooths post-meal spikes
- **Intestine:** improves stool consistency and bowel regularity
- **Microbiota:** serves as a substrate for bacteria that produce short-chain fatty acids, which are linked to the health of the intestinal lining
- **Cardiovascular:** higher intakes are associated with a lower risk of coronary heart disease in population studies

## How to increase it without discomfort

Increasing fiber all at once often causes gas and abdominal bloating. The best approach is gradual:

1. Increase by about 5 g per week, not all at once
2. Increase water intake in the same proportion — fiber without enough fluid can worsen constipation
3. Start with legumes: half a cup of beans already provides 6 to 8 g
4. Prefer whole fruit over juice, and keep the peel whenever possible

## Conclusion

Fiber is not a minor dietary detail: it is one of the few food components with consistent benefits in long-term outcomes. Reaching 25 to 30 g per day is less about discipline and more about how the plate is structured — legumes, fruits with the peel, vegetables, and whole grains present in most meals.
